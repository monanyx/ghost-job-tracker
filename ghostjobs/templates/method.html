How it works

Most companies run their careers page on a hiring system like Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters or Personio. Those systems publish every open ad as public data, usually with the date it first went live. Every night we read {{ n_companies }} of those boards and remember every ad we've ever seen.

The signals

Up for
The oldest date we can prove: when the hiring system says the ad was first published, or when we first saw it, whichever is earlier. If the same role in the same place was posted before under another ad, we count from that one.
Reposted
The ad closed and the same role in the same place came back under a new ad. Reposting resets the date most job sites show you.
Re-dated
Same ad, but the hiring system's posting date moved forward, so an old ad now looks new.
Says "talent pool"
The ad itself says it's for a talent pool, future opportunities, an expression of interest or an open application.

Ghost score

active 0–1Nothing unusual. stale 2Old, or one other signal. suspicious 3Several signals together. ghost 4–5Up for a year or more and reposted, re-dated or labelled as a talent pool.

Points: up 3+ months = 1, 6+ months = 2, a year+ = 3. Reposted once = 1, three times = 2. Re-dated = 1. Talent-pool wording = 2. Maximum 5.

Ghost index

One number per company from 0 to 100: half the share of ads older than 3 months, plus 0.3 × the share older than 6 months, plus the share of ads scoring 4+ (weighted 20). Only companies with {{ min_ranked }}+ open ads are ranked, so a single old ad can't top the chart.

What this does not prove

An old ad is not proof there's no job. Some companies keep "evergreen" ads open on purpose because they hire for that role all the time. That's still worth knowing when you're deciding where to spend an evening on a cover letter.
